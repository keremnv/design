# Answer

**No.** Meadow Creek ticket MCR-118 cannot be certified as the sole Prairie Gate source of mill receipt HFM-IN-604.

## What the records do establish

- **MCR-118** was received at Prairie Gate on 2025-09-07 (20,850 kg from M. Creek) and was moved into **South House 14 (bin B-14)**. The receiving hopper sheet for that move (BM-007) names MCR-118.
- **HFM-IN-604** is tied to Prairie Gate dispatch **PGE-OUT-5004** (manifest RL-9127, bill reference RL-5004-Q). That dispatch loaded **19,000 kg** from **South House 14** on 2025-09-07 and was received at Hearthland as **18,800 kg**.

So MCR-118 and the grain that became HFM-IN-604 both passed through the same bin. That is as far as the extracts support a direct link.

## Why sole-source certification is not supported

1. **The loadout was a blend with no ticket allocation.** The hopper sheet for OUT-5004 (BM-009) records **"no individual ticket"** for the 19,000 kg moved from B-14 to the load pit. The paperwork does not assign that shipment to MCR-118 alone—or to any single receiving ticket.

2. **B-14 held more than one ticket before loadout.** In addition to MCR-118 (20,850 kg), ticket **S-52** from Sunnyside Acres (15,900 kg) was also moved into the same bin (BM-008) before OUT-5004 was loaded.

3. **Ticket-to-mill allocation is left unresolved.** Under the yard-trace purpose, when a blended load is not allocated to individual tickets on hopper or loadout paperwork, that allocation must remain unresolved. OUT-5004 / HFM-IN-604 is explicitly in that category.

## Conclusion for the mill clerk

MCR-118 is a documented Prairie Gate receiving ticket that entered the bin from which PGE-OUT-5004 was loaded, but the supplied records do **not** establish that HFM-IN-604 came solely from MCR-118. S-52 was also in that bin, and the loadout hopper sheet names no individual ticket for the blended shipment. Do not certify MCR-118 as the sole source on these extracts.
